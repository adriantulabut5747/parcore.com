Start Here: Make Every Entry Cost Something

Most bases don’t fall to great attacks. They fall to easy ones: a straight path to the core, two Infernos inside one Queen walk, a corner where a Flame Flinger fires for free.

A good war base doesn’t have to stop every attack. It has to make every entry cost the attacker something: time, a hero ability, or a broken funnel. The 12 rules below do exactly that.

These rules are for war bases. The examples start at TH11, and most of the trap rules work at lower Town Halls too. To see them in a real base, open your Town Hall’s layouts:

Rule 1: The Real Secret: Dead Zones Break the Funnel

A dead zone is empty space you leave on purpose, inside or right next to the core. Troops reach it and find nothing to hit, so they have to walk around it instead of moving straight from one defense to the next.

That walk costs the attacker. The funnel splits, troops drift around the core, and they spend longer under fire before they reach anything that matters. Your key defenses get breathing room too.

Empty space doesn’t hide a defense; it only makes the path to it longer. Keep a few buildings outside the core to tempt troops away, and build the dead zone on the route troops will actually take, not as random empty squares.

Rule 2: Keep the Core Out of Queen Range

Archer Queen

The Archer Queen shoots over walls. If your core defenses sit too close to the wall, she stands outside the compartment and kills them without ever going in.

On a straight line, leave four open tiles between the core and the wall, so the wall sits on the fifth tile. On a diagonal, a gap of two wall pieces is enough.

Check every corner from the attacker’s side. One short angle can expose a defense that looked safe from the front.

Rule 3: Spread Your High-Impact Defenses

MonolithRevenge TowerMulti-Gear Tower

High-impact defenses are the ones that win the fight when they get time to fire. Never put two of them where one entry, one spell or one hero ability can reach both.

Fill the space between them with smaller defenses and storages, so attackers have to turn and walk between each one.

Rule 4: Put the Eagle Artillery Opposite the Town Hall

Eagle ArtilleryTown Hall

When the Eagle Artillery and the Town Hall are separate buildings, put them on opposite sides of the base. Town Hall at the top means Eagle at the bottom, and the same goes for left and right. If the Town Hall sits in the center, move the Eagle far enough away that no single compartment holds both.

From TH17, the Eagle is merged into the Town Hall’s Inferno Artillery weapon. Use the same spacing for your other high-impact defenses instead.

Rule 5: At TH11 and Below, Move the Town Hall Off the Siege Path

Log LauncherWall Wrecker

This rule is for TH11 and below only. From TH12 the Town Hall has its own weapon, which makes it a defense that belongs in the core, not a building you give away.

Log Launchers and Wall Wreckers head straight for the Town Hall. With the Town Hall in the center, that line runs right through the core and opens it up. Move the Town Hall toward a corner or an edge and the siege machine drives away from the core, breaking walls that don’t matter.

A war base protects the third star, not the Town Hall. Giving up the Town Hall’s star costs far less than an open core. If the attacker angles the siege at your one weak spot, make sure your defenses are aimed at that spot too.

Rule 6: Leave No Safe Spot for the Flame Flinger

Flame Flinger

A Flame Flinger parked outside your base chips away at defenses while the attacker sets up. Don’t leave it a corner where it can fire for free.

These can reach a parked Flinger: Mortars, X-Bows in either mode, defending heroes, the Monolith, the Revenge Tower, the TH17 Firespitter, the TH18 Guardian and your Clan Castle troops. Check their real range on your layout. A defense only helps if it reaches the exact spot where the Flinger sits.

Walk every edge of your base. If the Flinger can sit out of range and still hit several defenses, pull a defense closer or shift the layout until it has to move into danger.

Rule 7: Use Broken Walls to Steer the Army

A broken wall is a gap you leave in a compartment on purpose. It takes away the attacker’s clean Wall Breaker entry, but its real job starts after the first compartment falls.

Leave one gap where troops come in and another farther along, pointing away from the core. Once the first compartment is clear, that second gap and a building outside it pull the troops back out, instead of letting them break the next wall toward the Town Hall.

Troop targeting changes as buildings fall, so test it (Rule 12) and watch where the army actually walks.

Rule 8: Stack Red Air Bombs, Spread Black Air Mines

HealerAir Sweeper

Red Air Bombs deal splash damage. Healers fly in a tight group behind the Queen or the ground push, so stack your red Air Bombs on the route they’ll take and you can drop several at once.

Black Seeking Air Mines hit one target hard. Spread them across different air routes, so each one catches its own high-value target instead of wasting itself on the same group.

Put every air trap where air troops will actually fly: the path to an Air Defense, or the likely Healer route. Aim your Air Sweepers the same way, at the routes that lead to your key defenses. A trap in a corner nobody crosses does nothing.

Rule 9: Trap the Charge Path

Royal ChampionSpirit FoxDragon DukeIce Golem

Put Giant Bombs where the Royal Champion’s charge is likely to pass. From TH15, a tight group of Giant Bombs and small Bombs can kill the Spirit Fox and badly hurt the Royal Champion. The same cluster punishes a Dragon Duke charge.

At lower Town Halls, aim Giant Bombs at Hog Riders and Miners. Predict their path between defenses and put two or three Giant Bombs on it. That can wipe out a group or leave it too weak to finish.

Spring Traps go in the gaps along that route. They’re especially good against Ice Golems: a Golem that gets sprung before the core can’t freeze your defenses when it dies.

Rule 10: Put the Clan Castle Where the Fight Happens

Ice GolemWitchArcher

Most of the time, put the Clan Castle in the core, surrounded by defenses. When the attacker gets there, your Clan Castle troops fight right under your strongest defenses.

Ice Golems are the best guard for that spot. When they die, the freeze locks the attacking troops in place while your defenses keep firing. Keep them where the freeze covers the core, not on an edge where attackers can pull them out and kill them safely.

The other option is a Clan Castle near the edge, filled with Witches or Archers, to drag ground troops away from their planned route. Use it when pulling the army off course buys your defenses more time.

Rule 11: Spell Towers: Rage → Invisibility → Poison

RageInvisibilityPoison

Pick the mode in this order. Rage is the best default, because every defense around it hits harder while the attacker pushes through.

  1. Rage first. Put it next to the defenses that punish a push as it comes in: Multi-Infernos first, then Scattershots, Ricochet Cannons, Multi-Archer Towers and Super Wizard Towers. Leave out core defenses that are already strong on their own.
  2. Invisibility to hide and redirect. Cover the defenses you want hidden when troops arrive, and keep the next visible target far enough away that troops have to turn and walk to it.
  3. Poison to slow the push. Place it near the Clan Castle or a crowded defense area, so the Monolith and nearby defenses get more time to fire. It also slows a Blizzard once the Battle Blimp opens.

Place the tower on the path the attacker has to take to the Town Hall. In the right spot, it changes the timing of the whole push.

This order is for the popular meta bases. Every base design has its own best Spell Tower setup, so when a layout is built around Poison or Invisibility, use it the way the layout intends.

Rule 12: Test It Before You Trust It

A base only works if troops walk where you planned. Before you put a new layout up for war, have a clanmate attack it in a friendly challenge with an attack that’s common at your Town Hall, then watch the replay.

Check three things: where the funnel opened, which defenses fell first, and whether your traps went off on the right troops. Fix one thing at a time and test again.

Base Building Mistakes to Avoid

  • Every big defense in one compartment. One entry should never reach the Monolith, Revenge Tower, Scattershots and Town Hall weapon together.
  • A free Flame Flinger corner. If no defense reaches it, the Flinger takes your defenses for free.
  • A solid, even wall ring. It gives troops one clean route to the core. Mix compartments, dead zones and offset gaps.
  • All air traps in one pile. Stack the red Air Bombs for Healers; spread the black Seeking Air Mines.
  • Traps where no army walks. Predict the path between defenses and trap that path.

FAQ

How do you make a good war base in Clash of Clans?

Spread your high-impact defenses, break the funnel with dead zones and offset compartments, and put traps on the paths troops will take. Then test it in a friendly challenge and fix whatever leads straight to the core.

How many tiles should be between a defense and a wall?

Four open tiles on a straight line, with the wall on the fifth tile. On a diagonal, a gap of two wall pieces is enough. Either way, the Archer Queen should not be able to shoot a core defense from outside.

Where should you place the Clan Castle in a war base?

In the core, surrounded by defenses, so its troops fight under your strongest firepower. Put it near the edge only when you want Witches or Archers to pull ground troops away from the core.

Summary
  • The real secret: dead zones break the funnel and keep troops away from your core defenses.
  • Four open tiles on a straight line, the wall on the fifth; two wall pieces on a diagonal.
  • Never let one entry, spell or hero ability reach two high-impact defenses.
  • Eagle Artillery opposite the Town Hall until TH17, where it merges into the Town Hall.
  • At TH11 and below, an off-center Town Hall sends Log Launchers and Wall Wreckers away from the core. From TH12 the Town Hall is a weapon and stays in the core.
  • Cover every edge a Flame Flinger could park on.
  • Stack red Air Bombs, spread black Seeking Air Mines, and trap the charge path.
  • Clan Castle in the core with Ice Golems; Spell Towers on Rage, then Invisibility, then Poison.

A strong base makes the attacker earn every tile.
