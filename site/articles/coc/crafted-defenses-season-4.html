What Are Crafted Defenses?

Crafted Defenses are seasonal defenses built from the Crafting Station. Each season brings three new ones. You pick one, upgrade it, and when the season ends, all three leave the game and a new set takes their place.

  • One per base. The Crafting Station is free and takes a 3x3 space.
  • Pick one of three, and swap any time. Choosing or switching costs nothing.
  • Three modules each. Every Crafted Defense has three parts you upgrade separately, such as hitpoints, damage and one special effect. Each module goes from level 1 to 10.
  • Level 3 to 30. A new Crafted Defense starts at level 3 (all three modules at level 1) and maxes at level 30 (all three at level 10).

Season 4: Open From Town Hall 11

Season 4 opened on August 1, 2026 with three new defenses: the Cake-A-Pult, the Hot Candle and the Hero Hunter. For the game's anniversary, the Crafting Station is available from Town Hall 11 this season, the widest it has ever been.

Expected end: December 31, 2026. When the season ends, all three defenses are removed and the next set arrives.

The Real Secret: Hitpoints Before Anything Else

Most players upgrade damage first. But that is a mistake. A high-level Giant Arrow from the Archer Queen can destroy a Crafted Defense in one shot before it fires a single attack.

Archer QueenGiant Arrow

The fix is the Hitpoints module. Get your Crafted Defense above 2,400 hitpoints and the Giant Arrow can no longer snipe it. That means Upgrading to Hitpoints module level 4 (2,500 hitpoints), which unlocks at Town Hall 14.

Rule of thumb

Hitpoints module to level 4 first, every time. Damage upgrades are wasted on a defense that dies in one arrow.

The Cake-A-Pult launches cakes at ground and air troops. Every shot does damage twice:

  1. The cake lands and deals area damage. This first blast hits either ground or air troops, not both.
  2. It leaves a bomb behind that explodes 3 seconds later for heavy damage. The bomb hits ground and air troops at the same time.

The bomb is what makes it the best of the three. Slow troops and clumped support troops, such as Healers, take both hits.

Hot Candle Explained

The Hot Candle fires six flames at once, like a Multi-Archer Tower, and hits ground and air. It is strongest at the start of the attack. As the battle goes on, it melts and fires fewer flames:

  1. Full candle: 6 flames.
  2. First melt: 4 flames.
  3. Second melt: 3 flames.
Full: 6 flames
First melt: 4 flames
Second melt: 3 flames

Each flame always does the same damage. The melt only reduces how many flames it fires. The melt timer starts when the attacker deploys the first troop.

The flames split across the closest troops. With six or more troops in range, each flame takes a different one. With fewer troops, the flames double up. A lone hero takes all six flames at once, which is why the Hot Candle shreds solo hero charges early in an attack.

The Hero Hunter fires cards at one target at a time, ground or air. Every hit poisons the target like a Poison Spell, slowing it and dealing extra damage.

  • Heroes take double damage from its cards.
  • It prefers heroes whenever it picks a new target. It finishes its current target first, so it does not switch the moment a hero walks into range.
  • The poison fades fast. Most of its damage comes from the cards themselves.

How Module Upgrades Work

  • Upgrades need a free builder, like any building.
  • One module at a time on each Crafted Defense.
  • It keeps defending while it upgrades.
  • Boosts work. The Gold Pass builder boost, Gems and magic items such as Books and Hammers all apply to module upgrades.
  • Each module level gives 8 Sparky Stones for the Fancy Shop. A fully upgraded Crafted Defense earns 216.

Your Town Hall caps your module levels. This is the part most guides skip. At Town Hall 11 every module is stuck at level 1, so a TH11 player gets a level 3 Crafted Defense that cannot be upgraded at all.

What Happens When the Season Ends?

All three defenses are removed, along with every module level you put into them. The next season starts with a new set at level 3.

  • The Sparky Stones you earned stay.
  • Late in the season, think twice before a long module upgrade. A ten-day upgrade started in late December is gone a few days after it finishes.
  • Keep Hammers and Books for permanent buildings unless an upgrade decides your wars this season.

How to Beat Each One

  • Cake-A-Pult: keep moving. Fast troops leave the bomb's radius before it explodes, and anything inside 3 tiles is safe. Freeze it before slow, clumped troops walk into range.
  • Hot Candle: wait it out or spread out. It gets weaker every second, so send the main push after it melts, and never send a lone hero at a full candle. Freeze, Overgrowth and Invisibility Spells all shut it down.
  • Hero Hunter: give it a target before your heroes arrive. It finishes the troop it is shooting before it switches, so a tank in front buys your heroes time. Then leave it alone: anything that makes it pick a new target sends it straight to your heroes (see the mistakes below).

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Upgrading damage before hitpoints. Below 2,400 hitpoints, a high-level Giant Arrow takes it out in one shot.
  • Resetting the Hero Hunter onto your heroes. While it is locked on a troop, your heroes are safe. A Totem Spell, a Freeze Spell on the Hero Hunter, or an Invisibility Spell on the troop it is shooting all force it to pick a new target, and if a hero is in range, it locks onto the hero.
  • Picking the Hero Hunter for its name. Hero-heavy armies are common, but the Cake-A-Pult does more against everything.
  • Starting a long module upgrade at the end of the season. The defense leaves the game shortly after it finishes.
  • Forgetting the Town Hall cap. At TH11, the Crafting Station cannot be upgraded at all. Plan your builders around that.

Build the Cake-A-Pult. Hitpoints first. Enjoy it until December.
